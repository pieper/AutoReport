### Open-Source Frameworks and Platforms

*   **The interactive Medical Simulation Toolkit (iMSTK)** is an open-source platform that supports surgical simulation with advanced features like position-based dynamics, continuous collision detection, smooth particle hydrodynamics, and integrated haptics. It is compatible with engines like Unity and Unreal.
*   **SOFA (Simulation Open Framework Architecture)** is a modular open-source framework primarily targeted at medical simulation research. It allows for the combination of new and existing algorithms to create complex simulations, with early prototypes demonstrated for laparoscopic surgery.
*   **4C (“Comprehensive Computational Community Code”)** is a newly released open-source multi-physics simulation framework designed for complex problems in computational science and engineering, including applications in biomedicine like modeling human cardiovascular and respiratory systems.
*   **Open Source Physics (OSP)**, through tools like Easy Java Simulations (EJS) and Tracker, empowers educators and students to create and customize physics models and analyze data, fostering inquiry-based learning.
*   **Project Chrono** is a cross-platform, open-source physics-based simulation infrastructure implemented in C++. It offers an open API and a Python version (PyChrono), supporting a wide range of applications from robotics to granular flows, and is released under a BSD-3 license.
*   **PhysiCell** is an open-source, agent-based modeling framework for 3D multicellular simulations, featuring a library of sub-models for cell behavior and coupled with a biotransport solver. It is available under the BSD license.
*   **SimNIBS** is an open-source software package for simulating non-invasive brain stimulation, calculating electric fields induced by TMS and TES. It is copyrighted and licensed under GPL v3.
*   **MuJoCo** is a free and open-source physics engine designed for fast and accurate simulation, particularly for model-based optimization in robotics and biomechanics.

## Simulations of Human Soft Tissue (Surgery)

*   **iMSTK** is highlighted as an open-source platform for surgical simulation, emphasizing the critical need for accurate, physics-based soft-body mechanics and haptics for realistic tissue responses.
*   A new development in **open-source laparoscopic surgery simulators** focuses on improving soft tissue modeling within the virtual environment, integrating medical image reconstruction, mechanical property simulation, and user interaction design. This work utilizes methods like the Exoskeleton Structure (ES) and Equivalent Strain Energy Density (ESED) model.
*   **pyModSurgical** provides tools for modal analysis of surgical video to learn soft tissue dynamics, enabling the generation of realistic soft tissue deformation simulations that users can interact with.
*   Researchers at the University of Florida have developed an **artificial soft tissue** that replicates the feel and responsiveness of human tissue layers, aiming to improve surgical training by mimicking various tissue densities, resistance levels, and tumor sizes.
*   **Surgical Science** specializes in medical simulation, with a focus on mastering soft-body physics for creating virtual anatomical models that behave realistically during surgical tasks like dissection and suturing.
*   **CRESsim** is a realistic surgical simulator based on PhysX 5 for the da Vinci Research Kit (dVRK), capable of simulating contact-rich surgical tasks involving soft tissue and body fluids.

## Bioelectromagnetic Simulations (TMS, TES, PNS)

*   **SimNIBS** is a prominent open-source software package for the simulation of non-invasive brain stimulation, including TMS and TES. It allows for realistic calculations of electric fields and can optimize stimulation parameters.
*   Researchers are developing deep-learning-based methods for **TMS modeling** to accelerate the generation of computational models, aiming for more adaptive research and clinical protocols.
*   **NeuroSimo** is an open-source software designed for closed-loop EEG- or EMG-guided TMS, facing challenges in processing feedback within milliseconds for timely stimulation adjustments.
*   A robotic neuronavigation system with **freely available software (InVesalius and MarLe)** has been developed to improve the precision of TMS treatment by using robotic arms for coil positioning guided by 3D brain models.
*   A new technique, **TMS-iEEG**, allows for real-time measurement of TMS effects on deep brain structures by recording directly from implanted electrodes in patients.
